= Pytes JS3 Hybrid Inverter

Driver for the Pytes JS3 hybrid inverter (Modbus TCP, RS485 protocol Ver 3.2 register map,
input registers 33xxx, holding registers 43xxx/44xxx). All components share one Modbus
bridge and unit id; the inverter accepts only **one** TCP connection at a time.

== Components

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
|Factory PID |Natures |Description

|`Pytes.Hybrid.ESS`
|`ManagedSymmetricEss`, `HybridEss`
|The inverter itself. Receives the OpenEMS AC set-point, owns the work state machine
and the remote dispatch registers 44100–44108, does the dynamic feed-in limitation.
Battery and DC charger register themselves at the ESS (`addBattery()` / `addCharger()`).

|`Pytes.Battery`
|`Battery`
|Battery values from the inverter's battery port and the BMS (regs 33133–33150):
voltage, current, SoC, BMS current limits, fault words. `DcDischargePower` is
calculated from BMS current × voltage × direction. Also reads the backup port load
(reg 33148), because it sits in the same register block.

|`Pytes.Hybrid.DcCharger`
|`EssDcCharger`
|PV strings 1–4 and total PV power (regs 33049–33058), energy counters.

|`Meter.Pytes.Grid`
|`ElectricityMeter` (GRID)
|The inverter's own grid meter at the grid connection point (regs 33251 ff.).

|`Meter.Pytes.Backup`
|`ElectricityMeter` (CONSUMPTION_METERED)
|Loads on the inverter's backup port (regs 33137 ff., total in 33148).
|===

== How the AC set-point is applied

OpenEMS always hands the ESS an AC set-point for the whole inverter output
(`ActivePower`, register 33079, *including* PV). The ESS config `essSetpoint` selects how
this is translated for the inverter (register 44105/44106):

`BATTERY_CONTROL` (44105 = 2)::
The EMS computes the battery power: `set-point − PV + bias + losses + trim`, clamped to the
raw BMS limits, written to 44106 (negative = discharge). The inverter applies a constant
bias of ~190–220 W towards charging to the commanded value and conversion losses of roughly
30 W + 3 % of the total throughput (battery + PV) sit between battery and AC side; both are
compensated by a feed-forward, the residual by a slow integral trim (separate for charge and
discharge). Measured accuracy ±50–100 W, set-point reached after ~10 s (inverter dead time).

`AC_OUTPUT_CONTROL` (44105 = 4, recommended)::
The set-point is written directly and the inverter regulates its own AC output (PV/battery
split, losses and bias are its business). Measured accuracy ±20 W, reached after ~10 s, no
overshoot. Two things the inverter does *not* do in this mode and the ESS therefore does:
* It regulates only the **grid-side AC port**; loads on the **backup port** are supplied on
  top. The register value is therefore `set-point − backup load` (reg 33148).
* It does **not** know the OpenEMS battery limits (it charged with 55 A against a 40 A
  config / 50 A BMS limit). `AllowedChargePower` / `AllowedDischargePower` are reported
  AC-side (`limit ± PV`) and the set-point is clamped to
  `[PV − charge limit, PV + discharge limit − losses]` regardless of what a controller asks
  for. The lower bound can be positive: PV above the charge limit has to be exported.

`GRID_POINT_CONTROL` (44105 = 3) lets the inverter regulate the grid connection point with
its own meter. Not usable together with OpenEMS controllers (two controllers on the same
grid point).

Register 44106 is in steps of 10 W. The whole block 44100–44108 is re-written every cycle as
one FC16 frame: the inverter does not persist these registers and falls back to its own
self-use mode after the failsafe timeout (44101, 5 min) without writes. The same happens
while the ESS is not in work state NORMAL (e.g. the first 2–3 cycles after start).

== Values the inverter reports

* `ess0/DcDischargePower` is derived as `ActivePower − PV` (the AC-side battery
  contribution, what Sum, UI and all OpenEMS controllers use). The measured DC value is
  `battery0/DcDischargePower`; the DC energy counters use the measured value.
* `ActivePower` follows a set-point change after ~5 s, the BMS values after ~11 s.
* The inverter's own battery power reading (reg 33149) and the BMS calculation agree within a
  few watts.
* `getSurplusPower()` = PV + raw DC charge limit (for `Controller.Ess.Hybrid.SurplusFeedToGrid`);
  null while the dynamic feed-in limitation curtails PV (the measured PV is not the available one).

== Battery current limits

Four sources exist; the smallest value wins in `AllowedChargeDischargeHandler`
(measured 2026-09-17):

* EMS config (`Pytes.Battery` maxChargeCurrent / maxDischargeCurrent) - enforced by
  the EMS via the set-point clamps; only effective while the inverter follows the
  EMS set-point.
* BMS request (regs 33143/33144, "From BMS") - a request to the inverter; the BMS
  itself only protects with its hard thresholds (fault words 33145/33146).
* Inverter storage-control setting (regs 43117/43118) - the app's "max. charge /
  discharge current", 1:1. **This is the limit the inverter actually uses** when it
  runs autonomously (see below). It is read, never written.
* Inverter battery-model setting (regs 43012/43013) - 999.0 A placeholder with
  communicating lithium batteries; ignored when 0 or above 150 A.

The inverter exceeds its own limit by ~10 % for a while when the AC set-point asks
for more than the battery can deliver, so keep the EMS config at or above the
inverter setting - a stricter EMS value cannot be enforced in autonomous mode.

== Grid feed-in limitation (e.g. 70 % rule)

The limit is configured centrally in `Core.Meta` (`gridFeedInLimitationType =
DYNAMIC_LIMITATION`, `maximumGridFeedInLimit` in W). Controllers such as
`Controller.Ess.GridOptimizedCharge` keep the export below the limit by charging the battery
as long as the battery can take the surplus. Two mechanisms in the ESS handle the rest:

Dynamic limitation (`meter_id`, ported from the SolarEdge implementation of this fork)::
When the export at the grid meter exceeds the Meta limit, the ESS caps the inverter's AC
output (reg 43052, "limited power" in % of the rated power) at
`consumption + limit - 500 W`, where consumption = ESS AC power + grid power (house incl.
backup port). The inverter then curtails PV; the EMS set-point (battery charge/discharge)
stays in force below the cap. Grid and ESS power are averaged over 5 cycles, the register is
written only on change and at most every 10 s. The cap is released (100 %) when it would
exceed the rated power or when the inverter outputs clearly less than the cap it applies
(PV dropped), evaluated 10 s after the last write. Channels: `PvLimitActive` (INFO),
`AcOutputLimit` (W), read-back `LimitedPowerSetting` / `LimitedPowerActualValue`.
The inverter reports the active cap in reg 33121 (`OperatingStatLimitedExternal`, INFO).

Hardware backstop (`feedPowerEnable = ENABLE`)::
Additionally writes the hard limit from Meta (and a ripple control receiver, if present)
into the inverter (reg 44102 export limit switch, reg 44104 limit in 100 W steps). The
inverter then limits the export at its grid meter itself. Channels: `GridFeedInLimit`
(applied value), `GridFeedInLimitMismatch` (warning when the read-back differs for more than
30 s). The dynamic limitation aims 500 W below the limit, so the backstop only acts during
its ~5 s reaction time.

Measured behaviour with the backstop active (2026-09-17, limit 400 W, PV 5.9 kW): the
inverter keeps the export exactly at the limit, **ignores the AC set-point** and runs
autonomously in this order: export up to the limit -> charge the battery up to its own
setting (regs 43117/43118, exceeded by ~10 %) -> curtail PV. EMS-side battery limits and
OpenEMS controllers (Balancing etc.) have no effect while the backstop is active; this is
why the dynamic limitation via reg 43052 exists (measured: 43052 caps the AC output exactly,
20 % = 3000 W of 15 kVA).

== Settings that are not touched

The SoC limits (regs 43010/43011/43018) and the backup port setting (reg 43111) configured
in the inverter are authoritative; `setDefaultValues()` exists but is intentionally not
called.

== Modbus load

Every frame costs ~35 ms plus ~2.3 ms per register on this inverter. The bundle uses
5 HIGH-priority read tasks, one FC16 write and one LOW task per cycle (~560 ms of a 1 s
cycle). Changing the bridge configuration (e.g. log verbosity) forces a reconnect and the
inverter refuses new connections for ~60 s until the old session is released.
